## Core Enchantment Zone

**Slug:** `/zones/core`

The Core is the high basin of granite, larch, and turquoise lakes that the Enchantments are famous for: Inspiration, Perfection, Leprechaun, Sprite, Isolation, and the rest, between roughly 6,800 and 7,800 feet. It is reached either by the brutal 2,000-foot climb up Aasgard Pass from Colchuck Lake or by the long grind past the Snow Lakes from the east. Either way it is a serious day to get in with an overnight pack.

It is also the hardest overnight permit in Washington. For a party of two, the median day of the season predicts about 3% odds, and a typical July or August day is under 2%. The Core is the one zone where group size changes your chances: quota is counted per person, so smaller parties do markedly better, and a solo applicant has about three and a half times the odds of a pair. If the Core is the goal, the practical strategies are a solo or two-person application, a Sunday-to-Thursday entry, a June or October date, and two alternates in Colchuck or Snow that let you day-hike the basin from below. A Core permit also lets you camp in any of the other four zones.

Campfires are banned. Goats will follow you. Toilets exist at the larger lakes; use them.

## Colchuck Zone

**Slug:** `/zones/colchuck`

Colchuck Lake sits at about 5,600 feet under Dragontail Peak and Colchuck Peak, about four miles and 2,200 feet up from the Stuart Lake trailhead. It is the prettiest single lake in the permit area that you can reach in a morning, and it is the staging point for Aasgard Pass, which starts at the far end of the lake.

That second fact is why Colchuck is the second-hardest permit. A Colchuck permit lets you camp at the lake and day-hike up Aasgard into the Core, which is how a lot of people see the Enchantments. The median day predicts about 4%, and Friday or Saturday entries in July and August are under 2%. Sunday-to-Thursday entries and dates in June or October are where the better odds are. Group size does not change the estimate here.

Camping is on designated sites around the lake. The lake is usually frozen into June.

## Snow Zone

**Slug:** `/zones/snow`

The Snow Zone covers Nada Lake and the Upper and Lower Snow Lakes on the eastern side of the range, reached from the Snow Lakes trailhead on Icicle Road. It is a long approach, around six to seven miles and 4,000 feet to the upper lake, through open forest and burn. The reward is a base camp at about 5,400 feet from which the Core is a further 1,500-foot climb, and a permit that is roughly three times easier than Colchuck.

The median season day predicts about 11%. Late May and October are often wide open; July and August average around 6–7%. Snow is the sensible alternate for a Core first choice if you would rather approach from the east.

## Eightmile / Caroline Zone

**Slug:** `/zones/eightmile`

Eightmile Lake, Little Eightmile, and Lake Caroline lie in the drainage south of Icicle Road, reached from the Eightmile Lake trailhead. The walk to Eightmile Lake is gentle, about three and a half miles with modest gain, which makes it the easiest overnight in the permit area on foot as well as on paper. Lake Caroline is a stiffer climb beyond, with big views of Cashmere Mountain.

This is the easiest permit to win. The median day predicts about 24%, and the average July day is still around 16%. September weekdays average about 27%. It does not connect on foot to the Core, so it is a destination in its own right rather than a base for the through-hike, which is exactly why fewer people apply.

Much of the area burned in 2012 and the forest is regrowing; expect sun and little shade on the lower trail.

## Stuart Zone

**Slug:** `/zones/stuart`

Stuart Lake and Horseshoe Lake sit at the base of Mount Stuart's north side, reached from the same trailhead as Colchuck: the trail splits about two and a half miles in, left to Stuart Lake and right up to Colchuck. Stuart Lake is about four and a half miles from the car with moderate gain, a larch-lined lake under one of the biggest granite faces in the state.

Stuart is the second-easiest permit, with a median day around 15% and an average July day around 10%. Like Eightmile it is not a practical base for the Core, so it draws people who want a quiet lake rather than the through-hike. If the Colchuck trailhead is your plan and Colchuck itself is out of reach, Stuart is the alternate that gets you the same drive and the same first two miles of trail.
